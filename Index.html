The recovery strategy

Sort surplus by what it is

Not all leftover food is the same. Sorting it by type decides where it can safely go.

Raw & packaged

Untouched produce and sealed items. Safe, high-volume — the easy lane, and it feeds people.

Feeds people

Prepared food

Strict temperature rules apply. Some is recoverable with the right handling; much of it becomes compost.

Handle with care

What's left

Trimmings and anything that can't safely feed a person. It still has a use.

Compost, never landfill

Feed people first, compost the rest. That's the order the EPA's Wasted Food Scale prioritizes. Food donors are protected by the federal Bill Emerson Good Samaritan Food Donation Act and Florida Statute 768.136.

Where we stand

Jacksonville's gardens are legal in spirit, not in code

Jacksonville's Comprehensive Plan calls for encouraging community gardens as open-space resources that strengthen neighborhoods and improve access to healthy food. Its zoning code offers no business-as-usual path to legally operate one — not without rezoning, a state agricultural exemption, or political navigation.

Not in the code

Before planting, Overalls Farm's Nathan Ballentine asked the city what he needed to do to operate legally. He was told they couldn't say he could — and couldn't say he couldn't — because gardens aren't defined in the code at all.

$112 or $4,980

A Certificate of Use application costs $112. The city instead pointed Overalls toward a Planned Unit Development — roughly $4,980 depending on acreage, plus a full City Council vote.

2023 → 2026

Overalls Farm Three in Springfield applied in 2023 and didn't hold its Certificate of Use until 2026. Three citations along the way; the city eventually dropped all three.

Duval Local Harvest supports the Free to Garden Act. We buy from Jacksonville growers, including Eartha's Farm & Market. Growing food with and for your neighbors shouldn't be this hard.

What the act does. The Free to Garden Act is a proposed zoning ordinance that would make community gardens, market gardens, farmstands and on-site sales of what's grown on-site permissible by right in Jacksonville — with standards for hours, noise, odor, lighting and scale so gardens stay good neighbors. It also allows basic infrastructure and limited agritourism, and is written for local operators — eligible applicants must have beneficial owners or directors living in Duval County. The coalition credits the Duval Food Policy Council with convening the community conversations, the Duval Agricultural Council with engaging the wider agricultural community, UF/IFAS Extension Duval County and Blue Zones Project Jacksonville with research and policy insight during drafting, and SPAR and neighborhood leaders with the neighborhood-first perspective.
